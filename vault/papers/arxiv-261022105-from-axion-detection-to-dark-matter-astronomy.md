# From Axion Detection to Dark Matter Astronomy

**Authors**: Jae Hyeok Chang, Stefano Profumo
**Date**: 2026-10-15
**Paper ID**: [arxiv:2610.22105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22105)

## Summary

This paper proposes a novel framework for transforming a network of axion haloscopes into a global dark matter interferometer. By measuring spatial coherence and phase across multiple sites, the system can resolve the 3D velocity distributions and flow directions of dark matter substructures such as tidal streams and axion stars. A simulated four-site network demonstrates the ability to jointly constrain velocity dispersions, providing a new observational window to test axion production models through laboratory-based 'astronomy'.

## Key Contributions

- Conceptualized a global haloscope network to transition axion research from particle discovery to astrophysical characterization of dark matter substructure.
- Developed a mathematical framework combining interferometric phase, spatial coherence, and spectroscopy to determine 3D velocity vectors of axion flows.
- Demonstrated via a four-site simulation that longitudinal and transverse velocity dispersions of tidal streams can be measured jointly to test production scenarios.
- Extended the theoretical reach of laboratory detectors to include transient events like axion stars and minicluster bursts.

## Limitations

The effectiveness of the network depends heavily on the unknown local density and distribution of dark matter substructures like tidal streams.
