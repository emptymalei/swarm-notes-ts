# Testing compact binary nature in eccentric orbits with third-generation detectors

**Authors**: Syed U. Naqvi, Kaushik Paul, Chandra Kant Mishra
**Date**: 2026-10-13
**Paper ID**: [arxiv:2610.18833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18833)

## Summary

This paper investigates testing the Kerr nature of compact objects by measuring the spin-induced quadrupole moment (SIQM) in eccentric orbits using third-generation (3G) gravitational-wave detectors. The authors develop a post-Newtonian spinning-eccentric phasing model and perform Fisher-matrix forecasts for the Einstein Telescope and Cosmic Explorer. Results show that orbital eccentricity provides additional information that can improve SIQM precision, particularly for the Einstein Telescope due to its low-frequency sensitivity. The study also highlights that existing quasi-circular template banks are inadequate for eccentric systems, necessitating the development of more complex eccentric-spin template families for future searches.

## Key Contributions

- Extended the spin-induced quadrupole moment (SIQM) null test framework to include eccentric, aligned-spin compact binaries using a post-Newtonian phasing model.
- Demonstrated through Fisher-matrix forecasts that the Einstein Telescope (ET) provides roughly twice the precision in $\Delta\kappa_s$ measurements compared to Cosmic Explorer (CE) due to its superior low-frequency coverage.
- Quantified the loss of effectualness in quasi-circular template banks when applied to eccentric signals, establishing the necessity for eccentric-spin template families in 3G detectors.
- Showed that eccentricity does not uniformly degrade measurement precision but interacts with binary parameters to determine the ultimate sensitivity of gravity tests.

## Limitations

The study utilizes a truncated eccentric expansion and focuses on aligned-spin binaries, which may not capture the full complexity of generic precessing eccentric orbits.
