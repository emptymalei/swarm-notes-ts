# Galaxy-to-galaxy stochasticity has a limited impact on reionization and the 21-cm signal at fixed mean emissivity

**Authors**: Shihai Zhu, Yuxiang Qin
**Date**: 2026-10-14
**Paper ID**: [arxiv:2610.20184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20184)

## Summary

This paper investigates the relative impact of galaxy-to-galaxy stochasticity versus mean radiative emissivity on cosmic reionization and the 21-cm signal. Using the Meraxes semi-analytic model, the authors isolate these effects by varying scatter in ionizing and X-ray sources while maintaining or recalibrating the mean emissivity. The results indicate that most shifts in the 21-cm power spectrum and global histories previously attributed to stochasticity are actually driven by changes in the population-averaged emissivity. When mean emissivity is fixed, the impact of scatter becomes negligible relative to the forecast sensitivity of future observatories like SKA-Low, suggesting that mean radiative calibration is the more critical factor for 21-cm modeling.

## Key Contributions

- Systematically decoupled the effects of galaxy-level stochasticity from population-wide mean radiative emissivity in cosmic reionization simulations.
- Demonstrated that apparent changes in reionization timing and the 21-cm signal are primarily driven by shifts in mean emissivity rather than the redistribution of radiation via scatter.
- Showed that the impact of stochasticity on the 21-cm power spectrum falls below the sensitivity threshold of the SKA-Low observatory once mean emissivity is recalibrated.
- Identified specific regimes where ionization morphology is weakly affected by source population dominance and photoheating feedback.

## Limitations

The study is bounded by the specific source prescriptions and spatial scales resolved by the Meraxes semi-analytic model.
