## Summary

The paper presents the discovery and detailed characterization of SPICY 30021, a rare FU Orionis-type (FUor) eruptive young stellar object. Using mid-infrared (NEOWISE) and near-infrared (VVV) time-series data, the authors identified an outburst event and a preceding mid-infrared precursor, which points toward an 'outside-in' triggering mechanism for the accretion event. Follow-up spectroscopic observations confirmed the FUor nature through specific absorption features and high accretion luminosity. The study constrains the source's extinction, distance (1.7 kpc), and progenitor properties, contributing to the small population of known bona fide FUors and providing insights into episodic accretion in star formation.

## Key Contributions

- Identification and confirmation of SPICY 30021 as a bona fide FUor (FU Orionis-type) eruptive young stellar object, a rare class with only ~50 previously known members.
- Discovery of a mid-infrared (MIR) precursor in the NEOWISE time series, suggesting an 'outside-in' triggering mechanism for the accretion outburst.
- Characterization of the source as a highly obscured (Av = 12.5 mag) protostar with an accretion luminosity of 32 L_sol and a progenitor mass of approximately 0.9 M_sol.
- Integration of multi-wavelength photometric time-series (NEOWISE, VVV, VVVX) and ground-based spectroscopy (VLT, Magellan, SOAR) to reconstruct the spectral energy distribution and eruption dynamics.

## Limitations

Stellar mass estimates remain dependent on the choice of evolutionary models and the precision of distance measurements.
