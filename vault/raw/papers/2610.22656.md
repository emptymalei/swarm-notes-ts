Abstract
Heavy-tailed time series that exhibit regime switching are
common in environmental, financial, and engineering
applications, yet their statistical modeling remains
challenging because conventional emission distributions
— such as the Gaussian, Gamma, or Weibull — fail to
capture simultaneously the extreme right-skewness, the
slowly decaying upper tails, and the temporal dependence
that characterize such processes.
This paper develops a fully Bayesian Hidden Markov Model
with Singh–Maddala (Burr Type XII) emission
distributions (HMMSM). We derive the complete Bayesian
structure of the model — joint prior, complete-data
likelihood, marginal likelihood via the forward
recursion, joint posterior, and full conditional
distributions — and design an adaptive
Metropolis-within-Gibbs sampler that combines exact
Forward-Filtering Backward-Sampling for the latent
states, conjugate Dirichlet updates for the transition
structure, and adaptive Metropolis steps on the
unconstrained log-scale for the emission parameters.

1
Introduction
Heavy-tailed time series that exhibit regime switching arise
in contexts as diverse as extreme precipitation
(
Katz et al., 2002
;
Wilks, 2011
)
, financial asset returns
(
Cont, 2001
)
, insurance claim sizes
(
Embrechts et al., 1997
)
,
and reliability lifetimes
(
Lawless, 2003
)
. Three features
unite these phenomena and render their statistical modeling
particularly challenging. First, they exhibit strong temporal
dependence: successive observations are correlated and cluster
in time. Second, they display pronounced right-skewness with
heavy upper tails, so that rare but extreme events contribute
disproportionately to overall variability. Third, they exhibit
regime switching: the underlying data-generating mechanism
alternates between qualitatively distinct states, such as calm
versus crisis periods in financial markets
(
Hamilton, 1989
)
or dry versus stormy periods in
atmospheric systems
(
Betrò et al., 2008
)
.
Traditional approaches address only one or two of these
features at a time. Marginal distributions such as the Gamma,
Weibull, and Lognormal families can capture right-skewness but
ignore temporal dependence and regime structure
(
Katz et al., 2002
)
. Autoregressive models accommodate temporal
dependence but typically rely on Gaussian innovations, which
are ill-suited to heavy-tailed data
(
Cont, 2001
)
.
Regime-switching models such as Hidden Markov Models (HMMs)
can capture latent state dynamics but are often paired with
emission distributions whose tails decay too rapidly to
reproduce the extreme events that motivate the analysis in the
first place
(
Betrò et al., 2008
;
Chida, 2025
)
. Even heavy-tailed
alternatives such as the Gamma or Lognormal families, when
embedded in an HMM, provide only a single tail shape and
cannot adapt to the possibility that different regimes might
exhibit different tail behaviors.
This paper addresses the gap by developing a unified Bayesian
framework that accommodates temporal dependence, latent regime
structure, and heavy-tailed emissions simultaneously. The
proposed model combines a Hidden Markov Model with
Singh–Maddala (Burr Type XII) emission distributions and
implements a fully Bayesian inferential framework via Markov
Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC). The Singh–Maddala family is
particularly attractive here for three reasons. First, its
tail index
α
=

reproduce tail behaviors ranging from exponential to
polynomial decay
(

. Second, the

distributions — it reduces to the Weibull when

Third, its hazard rate can be monotone increasing, monotone

a

providing substantially more flexibility than the Weibull
and Gamma families that remain standard in the HMM
literature. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first
integration of the Singh–Maddala distribution within a
hidden Markov framework, and the first fully Bayesian
treatment of regime-switching models with Burr-type
emissions.
The paper makes three principal contributions. First, we
introduce the HMMSM model — a fully Bayesian Hidden Markov
Model with Singh–Maddala emission distributions — and
derive its complete posterior structure, including the joint
prior, complete-data likelihood, marginal likelihood via the
forward recursion, and full conditional distributions.
Second, we design an adaptive Metropolis-within-Gibbs sampler
that combines exact Forward-Filtering Backward-Sampling,
conjugate Dirichlet updates, and adaptive Metropolis steps,
and we verify its correctness through eight independent
numerical checks. Third, we demonstrate the framework’s
statistical validity and empirical effectiveness through an
extensive simulation study and a real-data application to a
26-year gridded rainfall record for the Republic of Ireland.
Along the way we demonstrate a weak identifiability
of the Singh–Maddala scale parameter in finite samples and
show that the model’s identifiable parameters remain stable
across chains.
1.1
Related Work
Hidden Markov Models.
Hidden Markov Models were formalized by
Baum et al. (1970)
and
popularized by
Rabiner (1989)
. The Bayesian treatment
was pioneered by
Albert and Chib (1993)
, who introduced the
Forward-Filtering Backward-Sampling (FFBS) algorithm for
exact posterior simulation of the latent state sequence.
Combined with conjugate Dirichlet updates, FFBS forms the
backbone of modern Bayesian HMM inference
(
Scott and Berger, 2010
;
Frühwirth-Schnatter, 2006
)
. Comprehensive treatments
of identifiability, label switching, and asymptotic
properties are provided by
Cappé et al. (2005)
.
Emission distributions for heavy-tailed data.
Gaussian emissions are inappropriate for strictly positive,
highly skewed data. Gamma and Weibull emissions offer greater
flexibility but remain restricted in their tails; Lognormal
emissions have heavier tails but still underestimate the
probability of very large events
(
Katz et al., 2002
;
Embrechts et al., 1997
)
. Distributions from the Burr family have been
used extensively in univariate frequency analysis of extremes
(
Singh and Maddala, 1976
;
Kleiber and Kotz, 2003
)
. Recently,
Unggul et al. (2025)
proposed the MSNBurr-HMM, which combines a modified
skew-normal Burr emission distribution with a hidden Markov
structure and estimates parameters via Baum–Welch. That
framework differs from the present work in three fundamental
respects: (i) the MSNBurr distribution is not nested within
the classical three-parameter Singh–Maddala distribution;
(ii) the inferential paradigm is frequentist
maximum-likelihood rather than fully Bayesian; and (iii) no
complete derivation of the posterior or of the full
conditional distributions is provided.
The Singh–Maddala distribution.
The Singh–Maddala distribution (Burr Type XII) was
introduced by
Singh and Maddala (1976)
for income analysis and
belongs to the broader Burr family
(
Burr, 1942
)
. Its
mathematical properties, including moments, tail behavior,
quantile function, and hazard rate, are documented by
Kleiber and Kotz (2003)
. Despite its extensive use in economics,
actuarial science, reliability engineering, and hydrology,
it has never been integrated as the emission distribution
of a Hidden Markov Model — a significant gap that this
paper fills.

6.5
Conclusion
This paper has developed and validated a fully Bayesian
Hidden Markov Model with Singh–Maddala emission
distributions for heavy-tailed, regime-switching time series.
The framework unifies temporal dependence, latent regime
dynamics, and heavy-tailed observations within a single
probabilistic model, and it provides a complete Bayesian
inferential apparatus for estimation and prediction. We
introduced the HMMSM model, derived its complete Bayesian
structure, designed an adaptive Metropolis-within-Gibbs
sampler, verified correctness through eight independent
numerical checks, and demonstrated statistical validity
through an extensive simulation study. The model outperforms
seven competing specifications by wide margins on a
simulated benchmark, and on a 26-year gridded rainfall
record for the Republic of Ireland it identifies two
persistent, seasonally coherent regimes with conditional
means of
87.4
87.4
mm and
134.0
134.0
mm respectively. We also demonstrated a weak identifiability of the
Singh–Maddala scale parameter, which we handled by focusing
inference on the identifiable parameters.
The HMMSM framework provides a statistically rigorous,
computationally efficient, and empirically effective tool
for the analysis of heavy-tailed, regime-switching time
series. This work may encourage of Bayesian regime-switching models with
heavy-tailed emissions and will encourage further research combining extreme-value theory, hidden Markov
models, and Bayesian computation.
