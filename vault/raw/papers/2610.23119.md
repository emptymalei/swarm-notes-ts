Abstract
We propose an online multivariate distributional forecasting model in which we condition the parameters of a vine copula on covariates. An online algorithm updates the parameters sequentially using only the most recent observations. Existing approaches require full batch re-estimation using all available data at each update step, which makes them computationally slow in large-scale forecasting applications and less suitable for settings in which dependence structures change over time. Simulation results show that the online procedure achieves forecasting performance comparable to that of the batch estimator, while reducing computation time substantially. We illustrate the method in a forecasting application to electricity net-demand across five regions of Great Britain and show that (i) the conditional vine copula model outperforms unconditional Gaussian and vine copula benchmarks and (ii) that online estimation improves forecast accuracy when batch re-estimation is not available. The algorithm is implemented in the
Python
package
ondil
.

Motivation
Multivariate probabilistic forecasting is central to many problems in economics and the natural sciences. It matters wherever decisions depend not only on the expected level of several related quantities, but also on how these jointly vary. Regional electricity net-demand
(
Gioia et al.,, 2025
)
, asset returns
(
Aquilina et al.,, 2022
)
, and weather variables
(
Jobst et al.,, 2024
)
are prominent applications. These multivariate developments are preceded by a broader shift from point to distributional forecasting
(
Gneiting and Katzfuss,, 2014
;
Kneib et al.,, 2023
;
Klein,, 2024
, e.g.)
. A typical example of such models is the generalized additive model for location, scale, and shape
(
Rigby and Stasinopoulos,, 2005
, GAMLSS;)
, which lets every parameter of a univariate response distribution depend on covariates. Extensions to the multivariate case do the same for the parameters of a multivariate Gaussian distribution
(
Gioia et al.,, 2025
;
Kock and Klein,, 2025
;
Muschinski et al.,, 2024
, e.g.)
.
Hirsch, (2025)
additionally considers a multivariate
t
t
-distribution. These approaches inherit the convenient, fully parametric structure of the Gaussian (or
t
t
) distribution, but also remain tied to a restrictive class of dependence structures.
One way to allow for a richer, non-Gaussian dependence structure is to model the joint distribution via vine copulas. Vine copulas decompose the dependence structure into a sequence of bivariate copula building blocks
(
Vatter and Chavez-Demoulin,, 2015
;
Vatter and Nagler,, 2018
)
. Letting the resulting dependence parameters vary with covariates has a long tradition in this literature, going back to the seminal time-varying copula model of
Patton, (2006)
. More recently, generalized additive
(
Vatter and Nagler,, 2018
)
and gradient-boosted
(
Jobst et al.,, 2024
)
pair-copula constructions extend this idea to flexible, nonlinear covariate effects within a vine copula. There, covariates can affect the entire dependence structure, including both its strength, through the dependence parameter, and its shape, through the choice of the bivariate copula family.
At the same time, the data such models are applied to have changed. Decisions in energy and financial markets increasingly rely on data that arrive continuously rather than in periodically released batches. For example, in energy systems, the number of smart electricity meters installed worldwide passed one billion in 2022, a ten-fold increase since 2010, with the number of connected grid sensors and devices projected to roughly double again by 2030
(
International Energy Agency,, 2023
)
. Financial markets illustrate the same shift at an even finer time scale.
Aquilina et al., (2022)
document that latency-arbitrage races between high-frequency traders occur roughly once per minute per stock, with the modal race decided within five to ten millionths of a second. This shift toward high-frequency, streaming, i.e., continuously arriving (pseudo-)real-time data is a defining feature of many modern forecasting applications more broadly
(
Gama et al.,, 2014
)
. It renders repeated full-batch re-estimation of a dependence model, using all historical data every time new observations arrive, impractical.
This motivates online learning, where model parameters update sequentially with every new observation rather than being re-estimated from scratch. The idea predates the recent surge of interest in streaming data by some time. It builds on the stochastic approximation method of
Robbins and Monro, (1951)
and the framework of online convex optimization in machine learning
(
Littlestone,, 1989
;
Shalev-Shwartz,, 2012
)
. More recently, online estimation has also been taken up within macroeconometrics, for instance for the online estimation of dynamic stochastic general equilibrium models
(
Cai et al.,, 2021
)
and for online learning in semiparametric econometric models
(
Chen et al.,, 2026
)
.
Closer to our setting,
Messner and Pinson, (2019)
propose to update regularized regression coefficients online by rerunning cyclic coordinate descent algorithms at each update step, using the previous estimates as starting values. Since the coefficients are expected to evolve only gradually over time, the updated estimates remain close to the previous ones, so that the algorithm typically converges after only a few iterations. This idea draws on recursive,
ℓ
1
\ell_{1}
-penalized estimation techniques from the signal processing literature
(
Angelosante et al.,, 2010
)
, which develops online coordinate descent (OCD) algorithms for such problems.
Hirsch et al., (2024)
and
Hirsch, (2025)
employ this machinery to enable online distributional (GAMLSS-type) regression, for univariate and multivariate responses, respectively.
This article makes two contributions. First, methodologically, we combine distributional forecasting using vine copulas and online learning, and propose an online, regularized generalized linear modeling framework for (conditional) vine copulas. In this framework, the dependence parameter of each pair-copula is linked to covariates through a generalized linear model
(
Nelder and Wedderburn,, 1972
, GLM;)
in Kendall’s
τ
\tau

Jobst et al., (2024)
, and updated sequentially via online coordinate descent with ridge-type regularization
(
Friedman et al.,, 2007
;
Friedman et al.,, 2010
)
. This allows for fast and stable updates even in high-dimensional covariate settings. To our knowledge, this is the first online estimation procedure for conditional vine copula models. In a simulation study, we show that this online estimator matches the forecasting accuracy of a repeated batch estimator for all but the strongest forgetting factors, while reducing computation time by a factor of up to 10. This factor is even increasing in the overall number of observations, since one update from, say,
1,000
1,000
to
1,001
1,001
observations is as fast as one from
10,000
10,000
to
10,001
10,001
but re-estimation from scratch becomes more expensive with more data.
Second, empirically, we apply our model to the setting of
Gioia et al., (2025)
, who forecast the joint distribution of regional electricity net-demand one-step-ahead in Great Britain employing a multivariate Gaussian generalized additive model (GAM) whose covariance structure depends on covariates. Using half-hourly data in a test set covering the entire year 2018, they only update their parameter estimates twelve times, at the beginning of each new month, along the test set. We revisit this setting replacing their Gaussian dependence model with an online, covariate-dependent vine copula model. This allows us to update the parameters also within months in approx. two seconds per update step in our most complex specification. We show that the conditional vine copula model outperforms two unconditional benchmarks, a Gaussian and vine copula model, in our forecasting exercise and that the proposed online procedure improves forecasting performance relative to the forecasting scheme (twelve parameter updates over the test set) entertained by
Gioia et al., (2025)
. The model confidence set of
Hansen et al., (2011)
contains only our conditional vine copula model estimated online with monthly batch re-estimation. This holds when comparing it to unconditional Gaussian and vine copula benchmarks across several forecasting schemes. More generally, forecasting applications of covariate-dependent vine copulas are scarce. In fact, we are only aware of
Jobst et al., (2024)
. Additionally, large-scale, high-dimensional multivariate probabilistic forecasting more broadly remains rare as well, where
Hirsch, (2025)
,
Muschinski et al., (2024)
, and
Gioia et al., (2025)
are, to our knowledge, the few existing examples.

Forecasting Regional Electricity Net-Demand in Great Britain
Forecasts of regional electricity net-demand, defined as electricity demand net of
embedded generation, are a key input for reliable and cost-efficient power system
operation and energy trading. Although such forecasts are often produced separately
for each region
(see
Hong and Fan,, 2016
, for a review on distributional load forecasting)
, many operational tasks, such as managing interregional power flows, and quantifying imbalance risk, require spatially coherent joint forecasts that account for cross-regional dependence. However, the literature on joint probabilistic forecasting of regional net-demand is scarce. In fact, we are only aware of the paper by
Gioia et al., (2025)
who model the joint distribution of regional net-demand using a GAM based on gradient boosting with a multivariate Gaussian specification in which the covariance matrix itself depends on covariates.
Conditioning the dependence structure on covariates in this way serves two purposes,
which we retain in our application. First, it makes the model interpretable. Time-varying dependence
can be directly related to physical weather regimes. Second, conditioning on weather covariates lets the joint forecast exploit this forward-looking information rather than relying solely on
historical dependence patterns.
The Gaussian specification of
Gioia et al., (2025)
, however, has two
limitations. First, it only allows covariates to shift the strength of dependence,
not its shape. This matters because simultaneous high net-demand events are
particularly relevant for security of supply. Since the
Gaussian copula has zero tail dependence by construction
(
Joe,, 2014
)
,
it can understate the probability of joint extreme net-demand outcomes. At the same
time, Gaussian dependence may still be adequate in less extreme or more regular
regimes and should remain available as a special case. Second, the parameters of the multivariate normal distribution are re-estimated only once per month in the test set so that intra-month
variation in dependence is not captured.

These considerations imply that a suitable model should be: (i) multivariate, so that aggregate and cross-regional risks can be evaluated coherently;
(ii) conditional on interpretable covariates, so that time variation in dependence can
be linked to economically meaningful weather regimes; (iii) flexible enough to allow
for non-Gaussian and tail-dependence while retaining Gaussian dependence as
a potential case; and (iv) estimable online, so that the
dependence structure can adapt as new observations arrive rather than being fixed
within each re-estimation window. Online conditional vine copulas (in combination with a suitable model for the marginals) satisfy these
requirements. They decompose the multivariate dependence structure into bivariate pair
copulas
(
Aas et al.,, 2009
)
, allow pair-specific copula families such as Gaussian,
Gumbel, Clayton, and rotated variants, and let the corresponding dependence
parameters vary with edge-specific weather covariates. Our online estimation
algorithm, in turn, updates these parameters sequentially as new data become
available. We will, thus, investigate in this application whether our proposed conditional vine copula model can improve forecasting performance compared to unconditional Gaussian and vine copula specifications. Additionally, we will analyze whether online estimation can enhance forecasting performance compared to a repeated batch approach, in which the parameters are updated only twelve times in the test set.

Conclusion
We propose an online, regularized GLM framework for conditional vine copulas, in which each pair-copula parameter is driven by covariates through a Kendall’s
τ
\tau
link. The model is estimated by a ridge-penalized online algorithm that updates parameters sequentially as new data arrive. Simulations confirm that the estimator recovers the true coefficients and that online updating matches repeated batch refitting at a fraction of the computational cost. Applying the model to joint regional electricity net-demand forecasting in Great Britain, we find that the conditional vine copula model using repeated online updating is the only model in the
90
%
90\%
model confidence set, and it is so for every scoring rule. Decomposing the comparison shows that the vine construction alone does improve on a Gaussian copula only mildly but conditioning it on weather-spread covariates yields significant gains across several scoring rules. In our setting, online updating is useful as a complement to, rather than a substitute for, periodic re-estimation.

C.2

Table

separates the cost of the batch fits, which include copula selection, from that of the sequential online updates. A single online update is fast (under a second for the Gaussian and unconditional vine copulas and around two to three seconds for the conditional vine copula).
