Abstract
External text can supply predictive information absent from numerical histories, but its value depends on how it is represented, combined and deployed. We present Attribution-guided Semantic Evolution (ASE), a modular framework for constructing textual factors, evaluating their conditional contribution, and deploying validation-selected numerical–textual predictors. ASE supports task-specific implementations with lexical measurements, semantic primitives or evolved event/state factors, coupled to suitable numerical experts. Its feedback-based implementation uses conditional refitting diagnostics to guide factor revision and combination search. We evaluate ASE on the four text-associated TESS datasets and dynamic CSI 300 stock ranking. The four forecasting implementations obtain test MSEs of 0.001041, 0.022439, 0.333115 and 1.8150, each below the corresponding published TESS score. On CSI 300, the frozen text-fusion implementation improves mean RankIC by 0.000642 over its numerical expert and by 0.000701 over matched shuffled text, with positive differences in all five seeds. Controlled text perturbations support functional reliance on text correspondence, while incumbent retention prevents regressions in the measured development criterion. Together, the experiments characterize ASE at two levels: forecasting performance of task-specific implementations and the conditional contributions of individual modules.

Introduction
Text-augmented forecasting requires more than retrieving relevant documents. A description of rising input costs may be useful when pricing power is weak, redundant when recent prices already reflect the event, or uninformative for the selected prediction horizon. The relevant object is therefore not a universally useful textual feature, but a measurement whose value is conditional on the incumbent predictor and its other inputs. Structured temporal primitives offer one way to bridge text and numerical forecasts
(
Li et al., 2026
)
; news-based factor extraction and reflection provide complementary approaches
(
Wang et al., 2024a
;
Wang et al., 2024b
)
.
We study a different feedback question:
what information should a forecast model return to the language model that proposes its textual measurements?
An aggregate score indicates whether a system improved, but does not identify which definition was redundant, which contribution depended on the current combination, or whether gains persisted across development blocks. Conversely, plausible verbal explanations need not describe what actually helped the predictor. The feedback-based ASE implementation links semantic revision to measured add/drop effects obtained by refitting the same predictor used for forecasting.
In the iterative implementation, the feedback loop alternates two operations. C1 proposes event and state definitions and compiles them into continuous similarity measurements and event–state products. C2 searches numerical–textual combinations under a fixed budget and returns conditional contribution and redundancy diagnostics. The preceding selected combination remains a candidate, preventing search from discarding a useful incumbent simply because a new round restarts elsewhere. The language model edits definitions; forecasting weights are fitted from numerical targets, with no language-model fine-tuning.
ASE organizes three modules: C1 constructs and revises textual factors, C2 evaluates their conditional combinations, and C3 deploys a frozen predictor or a validation-trained correction gate. We evaluate four-task forecasting and dynamic stock ranking as system-level applications, and use separate controlled experiments to examine the modules. Task implementations choose the factor representation, numerical expert and deployment rule to suit their data. Figure

Four-task forecasting.
We use FNSPID
(

Test MSE is normalized by the global variance of training histories. The main leaderboard combines published TESS/reference scores with locally adapted short-window models, including small PatchTST, iTransformer and TimeMixer implementations. It is a system-level reference comparison, not a claim of identical implementations, tuning budgets or paired predictions across every row. Task-specific model selection uses validation data; the iterative module studies use only its first 75%, reserving the remainder for assessment. The serialized benchmark rows lack authenticated dates/entity identifiers.
Dynamic CSI 300 ranking.
Historical index membership covers 627 securities cumulatively, with 299–300 valid observations per trading day. The numerical expert uses Alpha158 and balanced120 features. Training uses 2021, search early 2022, reserved assessment late 2022, and testing 721 label-boundary-purged dates in 2023–2025. Chinese announcement titles map to the next trading day; days without text remain included. Exact intraday timing is not authenticated. RankIC is the daily cross-sectional Spearman correlation between forecasts and realized labels, averaged over test dates. Five paired seeds (2031, 2037, 2053, 2069, 2081) are evaluated, with uncertainty estimated by 2,000 paired 20-day block-bootstrap draws. Training/search text coverage is approximately 20.4%/20.9%.

5.3
Module Analysis
C1: text correspondence.
In the iterative ASE implementation, frozen text permutations increase mean MSE by 1.60%, 1.34%, 0.43% and 48.78% in dataset order. Four of five seed-wise differences are positive in each of the first three tasks, and all five are positive in Bitcoin. This controlled perturbation supports functional reliance on text correspondence; it does not by itself isolate the benefit of factor revision.
C2: retaining useful combinations.
The restart-only search has 216 increases in the measured development criterion across 640 transitions; both incumbent-retention variants have zero. Retention therefore preserves the best eligible combination under the measured criterion. This is an optimization property, not a guarantee of lower future error.
Task-specific component comparisons.
On FNSPID, structured revision improves mean MSE over scalar feedback by 2.10% and free reflection by 2.17%, with 3/5 paired wins in each comparison. On Electricity, allowing numerical-input selection improves MSE by 2.81% over retaining all numerical inputs, with 4/5 wins. On Bitcoin, the complete product-bearing pipeline improves MSE by 3.94% over rerunning without products, with 3/5 wins. These selected task-wise comparisons illustrate conditional benefits: other tasks can favor reduced variants, so they do not establish universal module necessity. Full matched results remain in the research records.
C3: task-specific deployment.
FNSPID deploys its validation-selected lexical correction on every sample, while Environment activates its correction on 22.6% of test windows. CSI 300 deploys frozen aligned-text fusion, evaluated above with five paired seeds. These observations characterize deployment choices; a matched gate-removal experiment is not available. Complete task-wise controls are retained in the research records.

Conclusion
ASE organizes textual forecasting around factor construction, conditional evaluation and controlled deployment. Across four forecasting datasets, task-specific ASE implementations obtain lower MSE point estimates than published TESS scores. The CSI 300 implementation adds positive stock-ranking increments across all five seeds. Controlled module studies characterize the task-dependent roles of structured feedback, conditional selection and incumbent retention. These results support adapting ASE’s representation and deployment choices to the forecasting task.
Limitations
The leaderboard mixes published and locally adapted reference implementations with different backbones and selection protocols. Historical test exposure, unauthenticated chronology and Bitcoin’s 13 test windows limit generalization. The stock implementation’s numerical-reference interval crosses zero. Module effects vary across tasks; complete controls are retained in the research records, and the gate-ablation gap is stated above.
