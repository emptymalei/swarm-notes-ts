Introduction
Datasets for subjective tasks—emotion, toxicity, stance—are built by
paying several annotators to label each item. How many should each item
get? Should every item get the same number? If annotators differ, which
one should a given item go to? These are ordinary budget questions asked
on every annotation project, and they are usually answered by convention
(“three annotators per item”) rather than by measurement.
This paper studies one such question as a forecasting problem. Consider a
system that must predict a future annotator panel while annotation of the
current material is still in progress: some annotators have submitted their
labels, others have not. The available evidence is a
partially
revealed
panel, and the natural forecast target is the full count
distribution over the next item’s panel rather than a single majority
label. We call this
partial-revelation panel forecasting
. It defines
a sequential, cross-turn estimand: how much does an additional revealed
vote improve prediction of the next panel? This is related to, but not the
same as, the same-item annotation-budget question of predicting a remaining
vote for the item currently being annotated; we evaluate both estimands
separately.

1.3
What We Found, Including What We Did Not Expect
The axiom is natural and auditable, but it is not what drives predictive
accuracy: a per-level marginalizer that violates it matches or beats the
consistent operator. Consistency is better understood as a structured
regularizer—costly where per-level cells are dense, beneficial where
they are sparse—and as a deployment property, since one table then
serves every
k
k
and every panel size. Learning the completion helps, and an information set control attributes that gain to context entering the
completion channel rather than to added capacity. We also test the simpler
explanation that the cross-turn signal is affective inertia by conditioning
on partial majority and speaker transition. Because evaluator identifiers
are not consistently available across the corpora, we do not make a
general rater-routing claim.
We also report three findings that contradict claims we ourselves made
earlier in this work: exact-marginal training of the completion head is
worse than the cross-entropy surrogate it was meant to replace; the
apparent superiority of a Dirichlet head over a cross-entropy head is largely
explained by the likelihood family rather than the training loss in this
protocol; and the
usual oracle used to bound allocation policies is clairvoyant, so the
commonly quoted “fraction of oracle recovered” understates what a
policy achieves. Section
7
collects these.
1.4
Contributions
(i) The partial-revelation forecasting object, its cross-turn
conditional mutual-information characterization, and a direct same-item
held-out rater validation. (ii) Coarsening-consistent panel operators with a
closed-form Dirichlet-multinomial realization, an EM-inspired posterior-weighted moment estimator, a rater-count-invariant extension, a cell density shrinkage variant, and a
learned completion operator with an information set control. (iii) Ablation
and sensitivity evidence that probes the role of affective inertia and
speaker transition persistence, tests CCAR-simulated and non-CCAR masking, and compares allocation
against uncertainty and margin heuristics. (iv) A four-corpus evidence
chain with official held-out splits, cluster bootstraps, multiplicity-aware
reporting, and deterministic numeric, logic, and data quality audits. We
make no state-of-the-art claim; the contribution is a scoped measurement
framework and an auditable design principle.

Three practical consequences follow, in decreasing order of how confident

8.1

The held-out budget simulation finds positive saving for uniform allocation
over concentrated allocation in all 15 budget cells and significant saving
in 14. This operational result is more defensible than a universal
diminishing-return claim: fitted curves can violate the population shape at
sparse levels, as the

8.3
Predicting Per-Item Value Can Help, but the Reference Must Be

An amortized policy that regresses each item’s curve on
target-free features and then water-fills greedily recovers much of the
retrospective feature class headroom where the features carry item-level

targets and is therefore retrospective, not a pre-deployment ceiling. A practitioner should use a calibration-only cross-validation diagnostic to

illustrates why that check matters.
8.4
Identity-Aware Routing Remains an Open Problem
The theoretical

8.5
Modeling Guidance
For practitioners fitting panel
forecasters: choose the likelihood family carefully (the
Dirichlet-multinomial versus multinomial contrast is much larger than the
training loss contrast in these experiments); calibrate the concentration
post-hoc; and prefer a shared consistent table when annotation levels or
panel sizes are numerous relative to data, a per-level fit when cells are
dense.
