The calculation below addresses a distinct regime and question: finite eccentricity polar to radial fundamental frequency resonances are treated as a correlated multi resonance geometry, rather than as isolated circular epicyclic resonances, and are subjected to matched mass cross family tests and an explicit nuisance conditioning analysis.
The central question considered here is deliberately narrower than full environmental waveform inference. Different low order commensurabilities occur at different radii, so a correlated set of resonance locations samples the radial gravitational field at several strong field scales. We ask which local information about the matter distribution survives after the overall environmental mass amplitude is matched, whether that information persists across distinct profile families and regularizations, and how strongly it is degenerate with ordinary orbital parameters. The contribution is threefold. First, we recompute finite eccentricity resonance geometry with a perturbatively consistent first order expansion in the rotational parameter and explicitly follow the outer librating resonance branch. Second, we compare a horizon regularized Dehnen family with an independent Einasto inspired family while matching both the total environmental mass and the enclosed mass at a reference radius, and then vary the horizon taper to test sensitivity to the regularization. The resulting network shape coordinates are organized primarily, but not completely, by the local logarithmic enclosed mass slope. Third, we quantify degeneracy with eccentricity, inclination, rotation amplitude, and matter rotation using a conservative response matrix. The matrix is numerically full rank but strongly ill conditioned, which rules out robust local inversion from these conservative observables alone.
No timing or accumulated phase observable is used for parameter inference in this work. Peters type transport is derived in the weak field and has not been shown to provide a controlled subpercent systematic error for the present environmental strong field background; moreover, sequential resonance clocks are altered by resonance jumps and environmental drag
[

. These effects define the boundary between the conservative result established here and a future waveform level analysis.

Connection to Dissipative Inspiral Observables
The conservative result above is intentionally separated from inspiral timing. This separation is required by the current error budget.
First, the Peters–Mathews fluxes are derived in the weak field, slow motion limit
[
29
,
30
]
. Strong field EMRI evolution generally requires relativistic flux information; improved approximate Kerr schemes were developed to extend weak field transport into the relativistic regime
[
31
]
, and a relativistic perturbative framework for radiation in black hole matter environments has recently been formulated
[
14
]
. Recent matter environment calculations find that quadrupole fluxes can agree well with Teukolsky results over broad configurations, but they also compare post Newtonian and Teukolsky based prescriptions explicitly when modeling resonance crossings
[
19
]
. No corresponding subpercent error bound has been established for Peters transport on the environmental backgrounds used here. We therefore do not attach an inference precision to timing observables based on such a prescription.
Second, an inspiral does not cross successive resonances smoothly. Resonant self force harmonics generate changes in the orbital actions whose sign and amplitude depend on the resonant phase and forcing coefficients
[
4
,
7
,
8
]
. Such a jump changes the orbit entering the next crossing and therefore propagates directly into any inter resonance clock or accumulated phase ratio. Those observables cannot be used consistently here because the environmental resonant self force coefficients are not available.
Third, matter induced dissipation can alter the inspiral rate. Dynamical friction and accretion have measurable consequences in several dark matter EMRI models
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
. The effective stress tensor in Eq. (
3
) specifies the spacetime source but not the phase space distribution, Coulomb logarithm, capture prescription, or microphysics required for a unique drag force. Assigning a numerical drag correction from the metric alone would therefore add an uncontrolled model assumption.
For these reasons, timing ratios, accumulated phase ratios, resonance duration ratios, and detector precision forecasts are not part of the claimed result. A future dissipative extension should combine a relativistic environmental flux model with phase dependent resonance jumps and environment specific drag before these observables are used for inference.
