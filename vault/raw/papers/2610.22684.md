Abstract
Forecasting an unresolved event requires finding relevant evidence and deciding how strongly it supports each outcome. Yet outcome-based training of Large Language Model (LLM) forecasters often begins with preassembled evidence at a single prediction date, leaving evidence acquisition outside the learning loop. We introduce
AlphaBet
, an agentic reinforcement learning (RL) framework that trains a single LLM policy to search, reason, and report probabilities across multiple pre-resolution dates. Each dated forecast is scored by a proper scoring rule against the eventual outcome, allowing one resolved event to supervise decisions under different information states. We decompose forecasting regret into an evidence-acquisition gap and a probability-reporting gap, and show that Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)’s standard-deviation normalization can distort the reporting target under multi-class proper scores. On GLM-4.5-Air,
AlphaBet
reduces the Brier score from 0.731 to 0.626 on Forecast-Dojo and from 0.612 to 0.528 on FutureX. Controlled ablations attribute these gains to multi-date training and show that they come from improvements in both information acquisition and probability reporting. Our empirical results validate that GRPO’s std normalization degrades proper-scoring-rule performance while leaving top-outcome accuracy largely unchanged.

Introduction
Many real-world event forecasting questions, such as policy decisions or economic indicators, remain unresolved for weeks or months, while the relevant evidence continues to accumulate. Human forecasters have long practiced
longitudinal forecasting
, revisiting the same unresolved event and updating their beliefs as information evolves
(
mellers2014psychological
;
atanasov2020small
)
. LLM agents can retrieve and reason over evidence and produce probabilistic forecasts
(
futurex
;
forecastbench
)
. Recent LLM forecasting benchmarks have begun to assess this temporal dimension more directly, evaluating their forecasts at different points before resolution
(
futuresim
;
yuan2025language
;
forecastdojo
;
livemacro
)
.
Yet existing outcome-based forecasting methods still train only part of
this process. They commonly optimize probability reporting from
preassembled evidence at a single prediction time, leaving evidence
acquisition outside the trained policy and providing no learning signal
for how forecasts should evolve as information changes
(
approachHuman
;
outcomeRL
;
openForecaster
;
scott2026forecasting
)
.
Forecast-Dojo
(
forecastdojo
)
provides a replayable, temporally indexed forecasting research environment that makes this broader learning problem accessible. This motivates our central question:
how can outcome feedback train evidence-seeking and probability-reporting behavior across changing information states?
We introduce
AlphaBet
(Figure
2
), an outcome-based RL framework for agentic event forecasting. Using Forecast-Dojo’s temporally indexed environment, a single tool-using policy revisits each training event at multiple pre-resolution dates, actively searches and reads the evidence
available at each date, and reports a distribution over outcomes.
Each dated forecast receives proper-score feedback against the eventual
outcome, so one resolved event supervises decisions under multiple
information states. Cross-date memory is optional: the memory-free
variant starts each dated forecast afresh, while the memory-on variant
carries a belief notebook across dates. We show that the resulting forecasting regret decomposes into an information-acquisition gap and a probability-reporting gap (Proposition
1
). For policy optimization, we use GRPO
(
shao2024deepseekmath
)
without within-group standard-deviation normalization
(
outcomeRL
;
openForecaster
;
liu2025understandingr1zeroliketrainingcritical
)
. We provide a proper-scoring interpretation of this choice.
Extending the binary analysis of
bereket2025uncalibratedreasoninggrpoinduces
, we show that, at a
fixed acquired context, mean-centering preserves the expected reporting
gradient induced by a strictly proper score, whereas standardization can
reweight outcome classes and change the elicited probability target.
Empirically,
AlphaBet
improves forecasting performance on both Forecast-Dojo and FutureX. Our strongest variant improves GLM-4.5-Air from 41.6% to 50.5% accuracy and from 0.731 to 0.626 Brier on Forecast-Dojo evaluation set, and from 51.3% to 57.9% accuracy and from 0.612 to 0.528 Brier on FutureX, outperforming forecasting harnesses and prior RL-based training methods while remaining competitive with substantially larger off-the-shelf models. Multi-date training consistently improves over single-date training. Controlled evidence–reader swaps show improvements in both evidence acquisition and probability reporting. Finally, GRPO ablations support our proper-scoring analysis: within-group standardization degrades probabilistic forecast quality, while removing it preserves the intended training signal.
Our contributions are:
•
We introduce
AlphaBet
, an outcome-based agentic RL framework that trains a single policy to actively acquire evidence and report probabilistic forecasts across multiple pre-resolution dates.
•
We characterize outcome-based forecasting across evolving information states: a single realized outcome can properly supervise multiple dated forecasts, forecasting regret decomposes into information-acquisition and probability-reporting gaps, and GRPO normalization can alter the intended multi-class probability target.
•
We establish empirically that multi-date training improves forecasting across two benchmarks, with gains arising from both better evidence acquisition and better probability reporting. GRPO ablations further validate our analysis of normalization and probabilistic forecast quality.
