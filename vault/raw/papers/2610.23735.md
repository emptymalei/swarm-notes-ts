Abstract
Long-term time series forecasting maps a finite history window to an extended future horizon.
Many existing models focus primarily on the design of input representations and model structures, while paying less attention to the structural form of the history-to-future forecasting operator itself.
We examine this operator directly and find that, across model families, sensitivity matrices exhibit recurring diagonal patterns whose diagonals correspond to relative temporal lags.
Motivated by this observation, we develop a framework for direct multi-step forecasting based on lag-structured temporal operators.
Its basic building block, LagLayer, constructs a Toeplitz matrix by tying history-to-future connections with the same relative lag and incorporates horizon-wise and history-wise scaling coefficients to relax exact sharing.
This design enables LagLayer to serve as an effective replacement for temporal linear layers in existing forecasting models.
Stacking LagLayer modules with activations and multiple feature maps yields LagNet.
Experiments on long-term time series forecasting benchmarks show that our method is competitive with strong baselines, while sensitivity matrix diagnostics and limited-data experiments demonstrate that LagLayer provides an effective and sample-efficient alternative to a linear layer.

Introduction
Long-term time series forecasting (LTSF) supports decision-making in energy management, traffic planning, weather forecasting, finance, and industrial monitoring.
Compared with short-horizon forecasting, LTSF maps a finite historical window to an extended forecast horizon, placing greater demands on temporal modeling, generalization, and computational efficiency.
Recent methods largely differ in how they represent and mix historical observations.
Transformer variants target long-sequence modeling
(
Zhou et al. 2021

;

Linear and MLP models provide simpler alternatives for temporal mixing

;

)

Regardless of architecture, each forecaster induces a mapping from the historical window to the future horizon.
A complementary question is

Our contributions are summarized as follows:
•
We identify recurring lag-aligned structure in history-to-future sensitivity matrices across diverse forecasting architectures without explicit lag sharing. This evidence motivates a relative-lag sharing prior for direct multi-step forecasting.
•
We develop a model family around this prior. LagLayer combines a lag-structured matrix with horizon-wise and history-wise adaptation, while LagNet extends the operator through nonlinear depth and multiple hidden feature maps.
•
We demonstrate competitive performance against strong baselines on eight standard LTSF benchmarks. Ablations isolate the effects of adaptive scaling and nonlinear capacity, while matrix diagnostics and limited-data experiments provide complementary evidence for relative-lag sharing. Matched replacement experiments with existing architectures further examine operator transferability.

Representation and Architecture Design for Long-Term Forecasting
Long-term forecasting models have primarily advanced through new representations and architectures.
Transformer-based methods improve long-range modeling through sparse attention, decomposition, and frequency-domain processing

Direct forecasters provide a simpler alternative.
N-BEATS and NHITS construct long-horizon predictions through deep basis expansion and hierarchical interpolation
(

;
Challu et al. 2023
)
, while DLinear
(
Zeng et al. 2023

TiDE

)

Recent lightweight models further reduce cost through simplified mixing, sparse or frequency-domain parameterization, and factorized mappings
(
Chen et al. 2025
;
Lin et al. 2024
;
Xu et al. 2024
;
Ma et al. 2026
)
.
These works mainly refine input representations, feature mixing, or compact parameterizations, while the structure of the direct history-to-future mapping remains less explored.
We encode the recurring relative-lag structure observed in this operator’s sensitivity matrices through LagLayer and its nonlinear extension, LagNet, reducing the parameters required by each temporal mapping.
Structured Operators and Toeplitz Constraints
Structured operators have long supported efficient sequence modeling.
Temporal convolution reuses weights across relative local positions
(
Bai et al. 2018
)
, while structured state-space models admit convolutional representations for long-range dependencies
(
Gu et al. 2022
)
.
Toeplitz matrices provide the corresponding matrix form for linear time-invariant filtering
(
Gray 2006
)
.
Within this broader family, our setting differs from local temporal convolution: rather than sliding a kernel within a sequence, our lag-structured matrix directly maps a history window to a distinct forecast horizon.
Most closely related to our work, the Toeplitz Neural Network (TNN) employs relative-position-encoded square Toeplitz operators for within-sequence token mixing, focusing on computational efficiency and length extrapolation in general sequence modeling
(
Qin et al. 2023
)
.
We adopt a related sharing principle in a rectangular Toeplitz matrix, but derive it from lag-aligned patterns observed in long-term forecasters and use it to directly constrain the history-to-future mapping.
TNN therefore studies positional mixing within a sequence, whereas we study the forecasting operator between history and future.

Conclusion
This paper studies the structure of history-to-future forecasting operators.
LagLayer builds on a lag-structured matrix that shares parameters by relative lag and adapts along the forecast and history axes.
LagNet extends this operator through nonlinear depth and multiple hidden feature maps.
Matched operator-replacement studies on three established forecasters further demonstrate that relative-lag sharing transfers across decomposition-based linear, multiscale mixing, and frequency-decomposition architectures.
Across standard benchmarks, the proposed models achieve competitive accuracy; ablations, matrix diagnostics, and limited-data experiments further support the value of relative-lag sharing.
These results establish forecasting-operator structure as a useful design axis for long-term time series forecasting.

Appendix I
Limitations and Broader Impact
Limitations.
LagNet is based on a relative-lag sharing prior.
This prior is natural for many sliding-window forecasting settings, but it may be less suitable when forecasting relations depend strongly on absolute calendar positions, regime changes, or non-repeating events.
Adaptive scaling alleviates part of this rigidity through row-wise and column-wise scale modulation, but it does not fully replace explicit calendar modeling or regime-aware modeling.
The present study focuses on temporal operator structure; integrating LagLayer with dedicated cross-variate interaction modules remains a direction for future work.
Broader impact.
This work studies a general forecasting architecture and does not target a specific high-risk application.
Potential benefits include more efficient and interpretable forecasting models, which may help deployment in resource-constrained environments.
As with other forecasting systems, inappropriate use can lead to poor decisions when the data distribution shifts, when uncertainty is ignored, or when forecasts are treated as deterministic guarantees.
Applications in safety-critical domains should therefore include uncertainty estimation, domain validation, and human oversight.
