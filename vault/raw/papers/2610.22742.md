Abstract
We develop a sequential ensemble filter for reconstructing directed excitation networks from overdispersed multivariate counts observed on a regular time grid. The data-generating model is a discrete-time negative-binomial Hawkes process, for which we prove exact first- and second-moment stability conditions and derive a row-wise sufficient condition. The filter learns the network and the dispersion online, and a row-composition parameter forecast keeps enough ensemble variation to respond to structural change. In synthetic experiments, we demonstrate that a Poisson-based filter provides comparable point-estimate accuracy of the network, while the proposed filter provides substantially better calibrated predictive and parameter uncertainty. Under an abrupt redistribution of influence, the filter recovers connections that are absent before the change. An application to daily property-crime counts in sixteen Chicago community areas gives broadly calibrated one-step predictions and a recovered network consistent with independent temporal and geographic information.
Keywords:
Hawkes process, negative binomial, ensemble filter, network reconstruction, overdispersed counts, sequential data assimilation

The closest predecessor to the proposed filter is the ensemble Poisson–gamma filter (EnPGF) for Hawkes network reconstruction from count data

Note that the EnPGF assumes conditionally Poisson counts, whereas observed counts often contain additional variation from unmodelled heterogeneity, local bursts, imperfect binning or unresolved within-step dynamics. A conditionally Poisson self-exciting process is already marginally overdispersed because its conditional mean is random. Thus, the relevant distinction is conditional variation. Under a correctly specified conditional mean, the Poisson score remains a valid estimating function and the Poisson quasi-likelihood estimator retains the same probability limit
(
Fokianos et al., 2009
;
Christou and Fokianos, 2014
;
Ahmad and Francq, 2016

Davis and Liu, 2016

, but its model-based precision is too high, since a Poisson likelihood attributes more information to each count than an overdispersed count contains. This is consequential in sequential estimation because ensemble spread controls the next analysis increment. Overstating the information in each count contracts the parameter ensemble too quickly and eventually weakens its response to new information or structural change. We therefore use a negative-binomial observation model to represent the information in the counts through the ensemble spread. Related negative-binomial mechanisms for self-exciting counts appear in the INGARCH literature
(
Ferland et al., 2006
;
Zhu, 2011

, which excite both mean and dispersion. Multivariate count autoregressions that contain this recursion as a special case have been studied with conditionally Poisson marginals and contemporaneous dependence between nodes

Fokianos et al., 2020
)
and with conditionally independent counts whose variance may be quadratic in the mean
